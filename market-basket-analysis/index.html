EXECUTIVE RESOLUTION / WHAT WE ARE CHANGING

How we will grow small baskets without losing money on fragile returns

Over the past year, our online store fulfilled 19,773 completed merchandise orders worth £10.25M across 3,910 products. Right now, almost all of our revenue comes from large, multi-item orders. Baskets with 6 or more distinct products bring in 87% of store revenue (£8.89M) with a typical (median) order value of £322, and bulk orders of 31 or more products reach £551. By contrast, shoppers who check out with just 1 or 2 items make up 12% of orders and spend a median of only £58.

Our product pages currently show items one by one with no "Buy the Set for 10% Off" prompt or multi-pack option. Thousands of retail shoppers put a single popular item in their cart, such as a green Regency teacup, a jumbo retro storage bag, or a vintage alarm clock, and head straight to checkout. Yet when shoppers do buy more than one piece from those collections, they pick the matching color or companion piece 62% to 84% of the time. Putting a 1-click bundle offer directly on those product pages is the simplest way to turn single-item checkouts into larger baskets.

However, discounting our top sellers without checking return rates would backfire. Cancelled orders and returns cost the business £897K a year (8% of sales). When we checked returns for every product, we found that several popular ceramic and glass items break in shipping. Our #1 revenue item (the 3-tier Regency cakestand, £174K in sales) has a 5.3% return rate, our #1 most-ordered item (the glass hanging heart tea-light holder) sits at 6%, and our most frequently paired tea plates (Regency rose and green plates) have a 9% return rate on the rose plate. Promoting fragile glass and ceramic items in discounted bundles would only multiply shipping losses.

To protect our margins, we are putting a strict 5% return ceiling in place: no product with a return rate of 5% or higher can be included in a discounted bundle until its packaging passes a warehouse drop test. That rule holds back fragile ceramics and clears 5 durable, high-demand bundles across teaware, storage bags, alarm clocks, lunch bags, and playsets where returns sit between 1% and 4%. Launching those 5 bundles at 10% off adds £2,948 in net profit per cycle on single-unit retail orders, grows to £4,368 when paired with a 48-hour follow-up email, and reaches £24,528 per cycle when offered as multi-unit case-packs for our wholesale buyers.

Our Core Rule: Never discount a fragile item that breaks in the mail. By capping bundle eligibility at a 5% maximum return rate, we block high-return ceramics and glassware while capturing £2,948 to £24,528 in clean profit from 5 durable product sets.

STEP 01 / PREPARING THE STORE LEDGER

Cleaning up the invoice records: four reporting traps we fixed first

Before testing bundle rules, we checked the raw 541,909-line transaction ledger for reporting traps that could distort product associations and financial projections. We resolved four specific issues to ensure reliable numbers.

Keeping guest checkouts (saving £1.51M in wholesale orders)

Nearly a quarter of the raw records (135,080 rows) lack an account ID. Many reports delete those rows. When we inspected them, we found 1,371 valid guest orders worth £1.51M (15% of store revenue). Even more important, guest checkouts average £1,102 and 96 items per order, compared to £475 and 21 items for registered accounts. Deleting guest checkouts would have erased some of our largest wholesale buyers.

"Deleting guest checkouts would have discarded 15% of revenue (£1.51M) and erased our largest wholesale buyers, who average £1,102 per order compared to £475 for registered accounts."
LEDGER RECONCILIATION | 19,773 COMPLETED MERCHANDISE ORDERS

Separating credit cancellations (-£897K) and non-merchandise fees

Instead of throwing away the 9,288 cancelled invoice lines, we saved them into a separate cancellation ledger. That allowed us to calculate the exact return rate for every product and hold back fragile items (like the 3-tier cakestand with a 5.3% return rate and rose tea plates with a 9% return rate) from our bundle promotions.

We also separated 2,334 service lines for postage, bank charges, and account fees (£395K) along with 2,512 warehouse write-offs (-£22K) so delivery charges would not show up as suggested product bundles. Finally, we removed 5,268 identical duplicate rows (£22K) and mapped every product code to its clean catalog name.

THREE RULES EVERY BUNDLE MUST PASS
1. Under 5% Return Rate: Every item in a bundle must have a return rate below 5% so shipping damage does not eat up profits.

2. Profitable After Discounts: The added sales from new bundle buyers must outweigh the 10% discount given to shoppers who would have bought both items anyway.

3. Proven Buy-Together Habit: Bundled items must naturally sell together at least 50% to 84% of the time (6x to 34x more often than random pairs).

STEP 02 / WHERE OUR REVENUE COMES FROM

Moving shoppers from 1-2 items (£58 typical order) to multi-item baskets (£322+)

Our store records show a sharp contrast between small retail checkouts and multi-item baskets. Orders containing 6 or more items represent 78% of all store checkouts and generate 87% of all sales (£8.89M). Shoppers in the 6-15 item tier spend a median of £230, those in the 16-30 tier reach £336, and bulk wholesale orders with 31 or more products reach a median of £551.

On paper, 1-2 item orders show an average value of £307, which looks almost the same as 3-5 item orders (£316). That happens because a handful of wholesale buyers placed massive single-item orders (up to £168,470 on a single line). Looking at the median typical order value removes that distortion and shows the real progression for everyday shoppers: from £58 on 1-2 items to £141 on 3-5 items (2.5x) and £322 on 6+ items (6x).

STEP 03 / CHOOSING SAFE PRODUCT SETS

Picking matching sets that sell together up to 84% of the time without return losses

When we checked which products customers buy together across all 19,773 orders, clear collection habits stood out. When a shopper puts one piece of a popular set in their cart, such as a Regency teacup, a poppy playhouse kit, or a spotty paper plate pack, they also buy the matching piece 62% to 84% of the time (14x to 37x more often than random store items).

However, pairing items by popularity alone would have been a costly mistake. Several top-selling items and matching sets have high return rates because ceramic and glass pieces break in transit. We set a strict 5% return cutoff for all promotions: any product with 5% or higher returns is held back for a warehouse packaging upgrade, while 5 durable, low-return sets are cleared for immediate launch.

STEP 04 / TIMING & WHOLESALE ACCOUNTS

Weekday business hours bring in 78% of orders, European buyers spend more, and Q4 jumps 81%

Order timing and geographic patterns reveal clear buying routines across our customer base. Between 10:00 and 15:00 on weekdays, the store captures 15,375 completed orders (78% of total volume), driven primarily by small retail shops and business buyers replenishing stock during regular working hours.

Continental European wholesale accounts spend substantially more per order than domestic UK shoppers. Buyers in the Netherlands spend a typical (median) order value of £815 with virtually zero return loss (<1% returns), while Ireland (EIRE) and Switzerland spend £625 and £557 respectively. In contrast, Spanish accounts show an elevated return rate of 11%, signaling postal breakage or courier issues that require holding back deep discounts until shipping is resolved.

STEP 05 / FINANCIAL IMPACT & 90-DAY ROLLOUT PLAN

Our 90-day rollout plan: from £2,948 in retail sets to £24,528 in wholesale case-packs

To make sure our bundle discounts make money from day one, we calculated the exact profit left over after paying for the 10% discount on shoppers who already buy both items together. When we convert 25% of single-item buyers into bundle buyers on the website (Option 1A), our 5 safe bundles add £3,550 in new sales against just £602 in discount cost, leaving £2,948 in net profit per sales cycle (nearly a 6x return on the discount).

In Month 2, sending an automated 48-hour follow-up email to shoppers who bought only the main item lifts take-up to 35% and grows net profit to £4,368 per cycle (Option 2A). In Month 3, offering 4-unit multi-packs (+£11,792) and 8-unit wholesale case-packs (+£24,528) to our UK guest wholesale buyers and European accounts scales the program ahead of the Q4 holiday rush.

Practical safeguards

• Preventing Transit Damage on Anchor Sellers: Requiring reinforced packaging drop-tests on the 3-tier cakestand (22423) and tea-light holder (85123A) prevents shipping losses before promotional launch.

• Maintaining Positive Margin After Discounts: Sensitivity testing across 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% discount depths confirms that a 10% promotional incentive preserves healthy margins across all 5 approved bundles.

• Restricting Discounts in High-Return Regions: Spanish export accounts (11% returns) are excluded from promotional discounts until packaging and transit friction are resolved.
