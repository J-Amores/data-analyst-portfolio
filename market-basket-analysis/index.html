EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW / BUSINESS DECISION & NORTH STAR METRICS

How Our Merchandising Team Can Grow Small Carts Without Triggering Product Returns

Between December 2010 and December 2011, our online store completed 19,773 clean merchandise orders totaling £10.2M in sales. Because we sell to both everyday retail shoppers and wholesale gift shops, our typical order sits at a median of 15 items worth £302 (or an average of 26 items worth £518).

When we grouped orders by cart size, we found a sharp revenue divide. Large orders with 6 or more items make up 78% of orders and generate 87% of store revenue (£322 median order value). Meanwhile, 22% of our checkouts (4,354 orders) leave the store with only 1 to 5 items. Those smaller checkouts bring in a median of just £58 for 1 to 2 items and £141 for 3 to 5 items.

Right now, our store pages do not show 'Frequently Bought Together' prompts when a shopper adds an item to their cart. A customer comes in for a single green teacup or a retro lunch bag and checks out alone. At the same time, cancelled orders and customer returns drained £897K (9% of gross sales), which means we cannot blindly promote products that end up getting returned.

The Merchandising Decision & Our 3 Guardrails: Rather than running storewide discounts that give away margin, we checked which products customers naturally buy in the same cart across all 19,773 orders. Every recommended bundle had to pass three strict rules: (1) Return Risk Limit (each product must have an individual return rate under 5%), (2) Discount Profit Check (a 10% bundle discount must bring in more new cross-sell revenue than it gives away to shoppers already buying both), and (3) Minimum Order Reach (at least 300 real orders).

Executive Question Findings and Practical Resolution
What did we find? Orders with 6+ items generate 87% of store sales (£322 median order), while 22% of orders leave with just 1 to 5 items (£58 to £141 median order). Across the catalog, five matching product sets are bought together 6x to 37x more often than chance.
Why is this happening? Shoppers buying teaware sets, storage bags, or lunch bags want matching colors and styles, but without a "Complete the Set" box on the product page, 1-2 item shoppers miss the matching pieces.
What does it cost us? Small 1-2 item orders bring in a median of just £58 compared to £141 for 3-5 item orders (less than half the order value), while unscreened promotions risk amplifying our £897K (9%) return and cancellation losses.
What should we change? Launch 1-click bundle boxes for our Top 5 product sets at a 10% bundle discount (Phase 1), add a checkout-drawer prompt for 1-2 item carts (Phase 2), and build seasonal wholesale packs for European gift shops in Q4 (Phase 3).
OVERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Orders with 6+ items generate 87% of sales at a £322 median order value. Adding 1-click bundles on 5 low-return collections targets our 22% small-basket segment, capturing +£2,948 to +£24,528 in net store profit without increasing return costs.
CHAPTER 01 / DATA SCOPE & CAVEATS

Setting a Reliable Baseline: Four Reporting Traps We Fixed in the Order Ledger

Raw e-commerce order logs mix completed customer checkouts with cancelled invoices, guest carts, and shipping fees. Before testing product pairings, we cleaned up four reporting traps across the 541,909 raw order lines.

Why Deleting Guest Checkouts Loses 25% of Real Shopping Carts

One-quarter of our raw order lines (135,080 rows) had no Customer ID because the shopper checked out as a guest. Standard tutorials delete every row missing a Customer ID, which throws away £1.5M in real sales. Because every guest checkout still has a unique Invoice Number, we kept all guest orders when counting which items get bought together.

"Keeping guest checkouts preserved 25% of our real shopping carts (£1.5M in sales), while separating £897K in cancelled orders let us check the return rate of every item before bundling."
DATA QUALITY & CAVEATS CHECK | 19,773 CLEAN ORDERS

Separating Cancelled Orders, Postage Fees, and Duplicate Lines

Next, we moved 9,288 cancelled credit-note rows (£897K, or 9% of gross sales) into a separate return tracker so we could block high-return items from bundles. Finally, we stripped out 2,940 postage and bank fee rows ('POST', 'BANK CHARGES') so shipping fees never looked like a best-selling product, and removed 5,268 accidental duplicate lines.

THREE MERCHANDISING RULES EVERY BUNDLE MUST PASS
1. Return Limit: Every bundled product must have under 5% returns so fragile breakages never wipe out bundle profits.

2. Discount Profit Rule: A 10% bundle discount must bring in more new cross-sell sales than it gives away to people already buying both.

3. Minimum Reach Rule: Every recommended pair must appear in at least 300 real orders to guarantee commercial impact.

CHAPTER 02 / BASKET SEGMENTATION

The Basket Size Gap

Our store records reveal a massive revenue divide between small carts and multi-item baskets. Orders containing 6 or more items represent 78% of all checkouts and generate 87% of store revenue (£8.9M). The median spend for a 6+ item order is £322, which is 6x higher than the £58 median spend for a 1-2 item cart.

Shoppers checking out with 1 to 5 items make up 22% of all orders (4,354 checkouts), but bring in just 13% of store sales. Nudging a 1-2 item shopper (£58 median order) into a 3-5 item cart (£141 median order, 2.4x higher) or a 6+ item cart (£322 median order, 6x higher) is the single most cost-effective way to grow store sales without spending extra budget on customer acquisition ads.

CHAPTER 03 / PRODUCT AFFINITY & RETURN SCREENING

Top Product Pairs and How to Read the 3 Basket Metrics

When we analyzed what items shoppers add together across all 19,773 orders, strong companion purchasing routines emerged. In popular lines like Regency teaware, jumbo storage bags, and vintage alarm clocks, customers buy matching pieces 51% to 84% of the time, pairing them 6x to 37x more often than random chance.

3 BASKET METRICS IN PLAIN ENGLISH
1. Order Share (Support): "Out of all 19,773 store orders, what % of carts contain both Item A and Item B?"

2. Attach Rate (Confidence): "When a customer puts Item A in their cart, what % of the time do they also buy Item B?"

3. Pairing Multiplier (Lift): "How many times more likely are people to buy A and B together compared to random chance? (1x = random; 14x to 37x = strong natural bundle)."

CHAPTER 04 / DEMAND CYCLES & GEOGRAPHY

Fall Holiday Surge and Wholesale vs. Retail Markets

Store order volume and geographic patterns highlight two major takeaways for our merchandising team:

1. Fall Holiday Stocking (September to November): Monthly store sales nearly double from September through November as both gift shops and holiday shoppers stock up. Having pre-built gift bundles live on the site by late August captures that peak traffic.

2. UK Everyday Orders vs. European Wholesale Orders: While the UK accounts for the vast majority of order count, buyers in the Netherlands, Ireland (EIRE), Germany, and France place bulk wholesale orders that are 3x to 5x larger than domestic UK carts. UK shoppers need 2-to-3 item "Complete the Set" bundles, while European wholesale accounts respond best to case-pack volume discounts.
