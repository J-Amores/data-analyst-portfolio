EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW

Turning single-item checkouts into profitable multi-item carts

Over 12 months, our online store completed 19,773 clean merchandise orders totaling £10.2M in sales. Because we sell to both everyday retail shoppers and wholesale gift shops, the typical order sits at a median of 15 items worth £302 (or an average of 26 items worth £518).

When we grouped orders by cart size, we found a clear gap. Large orders with 6 or more items make up 78% of orders and bring in 87% of store revenue (£322 median order value). However, 22% of our checkouts (4,354 orders) leave the site with just 1 to 5 items. Those smaller orders have a median value of only £58 for 1 to 2 items and £141 for 3 to 5 items.

Right now, our store pages do not show "Frequently Bought Together" suggestions when a shopper adds an item to their cart. A customer comes in for a single green teacup or a retro storage bag and checks out alone. At the same time, cancelled orders and returns cost the store £897K (9% of gross sales), which means we cannot blindly promote products that end up getting returned.

The Merchandising Opportunity: We checked which products customers naturally buy in the same cart across all 19,773 orders, and filtered out any product with a return rate of 5% or higher. We found five matching product families where 51% to 84% of buyers already grab the matching piece when they find it, giving us a clear list of low-return bundles to feature on product pages and in the checkout cart.

Executive Question Findings and Practical Resolution
What did we find? Orders with 6+ items generate 87% of store sales (£322 median order), while 22% of orders leave with just 1 to 5 items (£58 to £141 median order). Across the catalog, five matching product sets are bought together 6x to 37x more often than chance.
Why is this happening? Shoppers buying teaware sets, storage bags, or lunch bags want matching colors and styles, but without a "Complete the Set" prompt on the product page, 1-2 item shoppers miss the matching pieces.
What does it cost us? Small 1-2 item orders bring in a median of just £58 compared to £141 for 3-5 item orders (less than half the order value), while unscreened store promotions risk amplifying our £897K (9%) cancellation and return drag.
What should we change? Add 1-click bundle widgets on the Top 5 product sets at a 10% bundle discount (Phase 1), add a cart-drawer prompt for 1-2 item checkouts (Phase 2), and tailor wholesale case-packs for EU buyers and Q4 holiday shoppers (Phase 3).
OVERVIEW TAKEAWAY
Orders with 6+ items generate 87% of sales at a £322 median order value. Adding 1-click bundles on 5 low-return collections targets our 22% small-basket segment, capturing +£2,948 to +£24,528 in net store profit without increasing return costs.
CHAPTER 01 / DATA INTEGRITY CHECKS

How We Cleaned the Order Ledger

Before calculating product pairs or testing discount models, we performed four practical data checks on the raw 541,909 ledger rows. Here is how any analyst can explain our cleaning process in 30 seconds:

1. Keeping Guest Checkouts for Basket Analysis (135,080 rows / 25% of lines)

One-quarter of the raw rows had no Customer ID because the shopper checked out as a guest. Deleting those rows would have thrown away 25% of our real shopping carts. Because every guest cart still has a unique Invoice Number, we kept all guest checkouts when counting which products get bought together.

"Deleting guest checkouts would have discarded 25% of our shopping carts (£1.51M in sales) and erased our largest wholesale buyers, who average £1,102 per order compared to £475 for registered accounts."
LEDGER RECONCILIATION | 19,773 CLEAN ORDERS

2. Separating Cancelled Orders & Returns (9,288 rows / £897K)

Invoices starting with the letter 'C' are order cancellations and refunds totaling £897K (9% of gross sales). Instead of mixing them into sales or deleting them completely, we moved them into a separate return tracker so we could check the return rate of every product before recommending it in a bundle.

3. Removing Postage & Accounting Fees (2,940 rows)

The raw ledger included non-product codes like 'POST' (postage), 'BANK CHARGES', and 'AMAZONFEE'. If you leave those in a market basket script, the computer thinks 'Postage' is your #1 best-selling product pair. We stripped all fee codes out first.

4. Removing Duplicate Lines & Fixing Typos (5,268 duplicate rows)

We removed 5,268 accidental duplicate line entries and standardized product names by Stock Code so a small spelling change over the year never split one product into two.

THREE MERCHANDISING RULES EVERY BUNDLE MUST PASS
1. Return Limit: Every bundled product must have under 5% returns so fragile breakages never wipe out bundle profits.

2. Discount Profit Rule: A 10% bundle discount must bring in more new cross-sell sales than it gives away to people already buying both.

3. Minimum Reach Rule: Every recommended pair must appear in at least 300 real orders to guarantee commercial impact.

CHAPTER 02 / BASKET SEGMENTATION

The Basket Size Gap

Our store records reveal a massive revenue divide between small carts and multi-item baskets. Orders containing 6 or more items represent 78% of all checkouts and generate 87% of store revenue (£8.9M). The median spend for a 6+ item order is £322, which is 6x higher than the £58 median spend for a 1-2 item cart.

Shoppers checking out with 1 to 5 items make up 22% of all orders (4,354 checkouts), but bring in just 13% of store sales. Nudging a 1-2 item shopper (£58 median order) into a 3-5 item cart (£141 median order, 2.4x higher) or a 6+ item cart (£322 median order, 6x higher) is the single most cost-effective way to grow store sales without spending extra budget on customer acquisition ads.

CHAPTER 03 / PRODUCT AFFINITY & RETURN SCREENING

Top Product Pairs and How to Read the 3 Basket Metrics

When we analyzed what items shoppers add together across all 19,773 orders, strong companion purchasing routines emerged. In popular lines like Regency teaware, jumbo storage bags, and vintage alarm clocks, customers buy matching pieces 51% to 84% of the time, pairing them 6x to 37x more often than random chance.

3 BASKET METRICS IN PLAIN ENGLISH
1. Order Share (Support): "Out of all 19,773 store orders, what % of carts contain both Item A and Item B?"

2. Attach Rate (Confidence): "When a customer puts Item A in their cart, what % of the time do they also buy Item B?"

3. Pairing Multiplier (Lift): "How many times more likely are people to buy A and B together compared to random chance? (1x = random; 14x to 37x = strong natural bundle)."

CHAPTER 04 / DEMAND CYCLES & GEOGRAPHY

Fall Holiday Surge and Wholesale vs. Retail Markets

Store order volume and geographic patterns highlight two major takeaways for our merchandising team:

1. Fall Holiday Stocking (September to November): Monthly store sales nearly double from September through November as both gift shops and holiday shoppers stock up. Having pre-built gift bundles live on the site by late August captures that peak traffic.

2. UK Everyday Orders vs. European Wholesale Orders: While the UK accounts for the vast majority of order count, buyers in the Netherlands, Ireland (EIRE), Germany, and France place bulk wholesale orders that are 3x to 5x larger than domestic UK carts. UK shoppers need 2-to-3 item "Complete the Set" bundles, while European wholesale accounts respond best to case-pack volume discounts.
