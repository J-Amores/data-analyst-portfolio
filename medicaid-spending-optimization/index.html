Why we ran this review OUTPATIENT BUDGET REVIEW
  • Between 2020 and 2024, medication costs across our $85.0M outpatient pharmacy budget rose 45.6%, while insurance reimbursement rates stayed flat under bundled payment contracts.
  • The Director of Pharmacy, CFO, and P&T Committee asked our team to review five years of prescription claims to find out why spending kept climbing and where the hospital could recover margin.
  • We focused on three questions: whether cost growth came from treating more patients or higher drug prices, where brand-name drugs were still dispensed over generics, and how much prices varied across generic suppliers.

Out of nearly 4,800 medications, just 5% (239 drugs) account for 72.2% ($61.35M) of the hospital's entire outpatient drug spend.

A single list of 25 medications accounts for 32.2% ($27.33M) of everything we spend, led by Humira, Ozempic, Biktarvy, Jardiance, and Trulicity.

Broad budget cuts across thousands of cheap floor-stock generics create friction for clinical teams without helping hospital margins. Formulary reviews must focus strictly on high-yield lines.

On mature brand and specialty drugs, manufacturers raised unit acquisition prices even when prescription order counts stayed flat or fell.

Key examples include Xifaxan (74.1% of spend growth from price hikes), Emflaza (66%), Trulicity (unit price up 106.6%), Enbrel (56%), Cosentyx (55%), and Vyvanse (unit price rose 37% while volume dropped 15%).

Formulary action: Enforce automatic substitution toward bioequivalent generics and biosimilars to eliminate unnecessary brand premiums.

Higher patient demand for newer treatments (76.1% of high-growth drugs) PATIENT DEMAND
CLINICAL UTILIZATION EXPANSION ENGINE
76.1% of High-Growth Drugs
76.1% VOLUME-LED GROWTH ENGINE

Newer diabetes, weight management, and immunology therapies accelerated because physicians prescribed them across expanded patient cohorts.

Ozempic prescriptions grew +928.7%, Jardiance prescriptions climbed +257%, and Dupixent order counts increased over 130-fold.

Formulary action: Implement EHR order checks in Epic and Cerner to verify guideline-directed first-line therapies prior to dispensing higher-tier lines.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Leadership cannot use a one-size-fits-all policy. Separating unit price hikes from patient volume growth directs the P&T Committee precisely where to apply generic switches versus clinical order set pathways.

Supplier contract locks and automatic generic substitution recover $3.2M annually

Capturing an 82% conversion rate across $3.89M in identified annual overpayments with clinical guardrails

Projected savings waterfall +$2.10M SUPPLIER LOCKS + $1.10M GENERIC SUBSTITUTION = $3.20M NET
How we recover the $3.2M safely POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Lock in primary GPO generic suppliers (+$2.10M): Lock wholesaler ordering profiles to our contracted 25th-percentile supplier tiers. Requires zero changes to how physicians write prescriptions.

2. Turn on automatic EHR generic substitution (+$1.10M): Automatically switch off-patent brand orders at order entry to bioequivalent generics in stock (Vyvanse $472K, Concerta $367K, Spiriva $176K, Advair $136K, Banzel $97K, Jornay PM $80K).

Net annual margin recovery: $3.20M at full adoption ($2.56M at our 80% Year 1 target). Total annual overpayment identified across multi-source medications is $3.89M.

Patient therapy changes stay at 1.8% (strictly below our 2.5% chronic care safety limit), and post-filter outlier risk is 0.0% (strictly below 5.0% limit).
