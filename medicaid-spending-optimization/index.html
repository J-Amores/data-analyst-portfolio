Five-year review of hospital outpatient and discharge medication spending

$3.20M in annual margin recovery
Why we ran this review OUTPATIENT BUDGET REVIEW
  • Between 2020 and 2024, medication costs across our $85.0M outpatient pharmacy budget rose 45.6%, while insurance reimbursement rates stayed flat under bundled payment contracts.
  • The Director of Pharmacy, CFO, and P&T Committee asked our team to review five years of prescription claims to find out why spending kept climbing and where the hospital could recover margin.
  • We focused on three questions: whether cost growth came from treating more patients or higher drug prices, where brand-name drugs were still dispensed over generics, and how much prices varied across generic suppliers.

Key findings from spend concentration BUDGET BREAKDOWN

Out of nearly 4,800 medications, just 5% (239 drugs) account for 72.2% ($61.35M) of the hospital's entire outpatient drug spend.

Expanding to the top 10% (478 drugs) covers 84.9% ($72.17M) of the budget, leaving the other 90% of routine generic medications (over 4,200 drugs) at just 15.1%.

A single list of 25 medications accounts for 32.2% ($27.33M) of everything we spend, led by Humira, Ozempic, Biktarvy, Jardiance, and Trulicity.

Broad budget cuts across thousands of cheap floor-stock generics create headaches for nurses and doctors without helping hospital margins. Formulary reviews should focus on these top 25 medications.

On older brand and specialty drugs, manufacturers raised unit prices even when prescription volume stayed flat or fell.

Key examples include Xifaxan (74% of spend growth from price hikes), Emflaza (66%), Trulicity (unit price up 107%), Enbrel (56%), Cosentyx (55%), and Vyvanse (unit price rose 37% while volume dropped 15%).

What to do: Switch prescribers toward lower-cost generic, biosimilar, or therapeutic alternatives.

Higher patient demand for newer treatments (76.1% of high-growth drugs) PATIENT DEMAND
CLINICAL UTILIZATION EXPANSION
76.1% of High-Growth Drugs
76.1% VOLUME-LED GROWTH ENGINE

Newer diabetes and immunology treatments grew because doctors prescribed them to far more patients.

Ozempic prescriptions grew 929%, Jardiance prescriptions grew 257%, and Dupixent prescriptions grew over 130-fold.

What to do: Add clinical order checks in Epic and Cerner to confirm first-line treatments were tried first.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Hospital leadership cannot use the same rule for both groups. Separating price hikes from patient demand shows the P&T Committee where to use generic switches versus clinical order sets.

Key purchasing review findings PURCHASING AUDIT

Competing manufacturers charge a 71.6% typical price spread between higher-cost and lower-cost manufacturers (75th vs. 25th percentile) for the exact same generic pill.

Nearly two-thirds (65.8%) of multi-source generics have a price gap greater than 35% across competing suppliers after excluding long-acting injectables.

Unnecessary brand dispensing continues in outpatient and discharge pharmacies for off-patent drugs like Vyvanse, Concerta, Spiriva Respimat, Advair Diskus, and Suboxone even when FDA-approved generics are in stock.

When pharmacy buyers order stock without locking in our primary contracted supplier, the hospital pays a steep markup for the identical active ingredient.

Supplier contract locks and automatic generic substitution recover $3.2M annually

Capturing an 82% conversion rate across $3.89M in annual overpayments

Projected savings waterfall +$2.1M SUPPLIER LOCKS + $1.1M GENERIC SUBSTITUTION = $3.2M NET
How we recover the $3.2M safely POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Lock in primary GPO generic suppliers (+$2.10M): Lock wholesaler ordering profiles to our 25th-percentile contracted generic suppliers. Requires zero changes to how doctors write prescriptions.

2. Turn on automatic EHR generic substitution (+$1.10M): Automatically switch off-patent brand orders at order entry to bioequivalent generics in stock, led by Vyvanse ($472K), Concerta ($367K), Spiriva Respimat ($176K), and Advair Diskus ($136K).

Net annual margin recovery: $3.20M at full adoption ($2.56M at our 80% Year 1 target). Total annual overpayment identified across multi-source medications is $3.89M.

Patient therapy changes stay at 1.8% (strictly below our 2.5% chronic care safety limit), and doctors always keep a one-click override when a specific brand is medically necessary.
