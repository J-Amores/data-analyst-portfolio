Executive summary and 4-question overview

Section 01

Between May 2008 and November 2010, our call center dialed 105,754 outbound calls across 41,188 prospects to sell term deposits. Overall, 4,640 customers signed up, which comes out to an 11.27% conversion rate per lead and 4.39 sign-ups for every 100 phone calls.

When we broke down performance by call attempt, we found a major drain on agent time. Calls 1 through 3 brought in 88.04% of all sign-ups (4,085 deposits). However, reps kept dialing unresponsive prospects up to 56 times in a single campaign. Nearly half of all outbound calls (50,949 dials, or 48.18% of call center volume) went to prospects on their 4th through 56th attempt. Those extra calls produced only 555 sign-ups, dropping our yield per call from 7.45% down to 1.09% and increasing agent talk time per sign-up by 41.0% (from 36.4 minutes to 51.4 minutes).

At the same time, our call lists were aimed at the wrong groups. More than half of all calls (56,174 dials) went to working-age adults between 31 and 45, who converted at just 9.37%. Seniors aged 60 and older converted at 45.49%, students converted at 31.43%, and customers who signed up in a previous campaign converted at 65.11%. Mobile phone outreach also beat landline calls by nearly three to one (14.74% vs. 5.23%).

Stopping repeat outreach after 3 calls frees up 28,044 to 50,949 phone calls (475.2 hours of agent talk time) while keeping 88.04% of our current sign-ups, staying safely above leadership's 85.0% volume goal. Redirecting those saved calls to mobile numbers, past buyers, and high-converting age groups adds 1,535 to 3,242 net new term deposits (+33.1% to +69.9%) using our existing staff.

Executive Question Findings and Recommended Action
What did we find? Attempts 1 to 3 capture 88.04% of sign-ups (4,085 deposits). Attempts 4 to 56 take up 48.18% of all phone calls (50,949 dials) but bring in only 11.96% of sign-ups.
Why is this happening? The dialer has no cap on repeat calls, and 53.1% of calls go to 31-45 year olds (9.37% conversion) or landline phones (5.23% conversion) instead of mobile numbers (14.74%), seniors (45.49%), and past buyers (65.11%).
What does it cost us? Agents spend 475.2 hours of talk time on 4+ call attempts. Winning a sign-up on attempts 4+ takes 51.37 minutes of phone time compared to 36.42 minutes on attempts 1 to 3 (+41.0% higher labor cost).
What should we change? Cap outreach at 3 calls per prospect (Phase 1), route calls to mobile numbers and recent leads within 7 days (Phase 2), and shift call lists toward seniors, students, and low-rate periods (Phase 3).

How we prepared the data (avoiding common reporting traps)

Section 02

Before testing policy changes, we audited the raw campaign files to prevent data errors and unrealistic projections. We resolved four critical technical traps that commonly lead to flawed call center recommendations:

Data Preparation Check Plain-English Explanation and Operational Safeguard
Separating call length from targeting rules A common mistake with this dataset is using call duration to predict who will buy. Because we only know how long a call lasted after the customer hangs up, we removed call duration from our targeting model and used it only to measure how many hours agents spent on the phone.
Handling uncontacted prospects (code 999) In the raw file, 39,673 prospects (96.32%) had '999' listed for days since last contact. Instead of treating 999 as roughly three years, we separated prospects into two clear groups: never contacted before (9.26% conversion) and previously contacted (63.83% conversion).
Keeping incomplete records visible Rather than deleting or guessing missing values, we tracked the 10,700 prospects who had 'unknown' listed on their credit or background fields. Those incomplete profiles converted at just 7.30% compared to 12.66% for complete profiles (a 1.73x drop).
Checking account balances across both bank files The newer 41,188-row file adds interest rate data, while the older 45,211-row file includes yearly bank balances. Checking the older file confirmed that customers with more than the 448 EUR median balance convert at 14.33% vs. 9.06% for lower balances (1.58x higher).

Attempts 4 and up use nearly half our phone calls for just 12% of sign-ups

Section 03

Our analysis uncovered an immediate operational bottleneck: outbound agents continue dialing non-responsive prospects up to 56 times in a single campaign cycle. While the first 3 call attempts capture 88.04% of total deposits, attempts 4 through 56 consume 48.18% of all outbound calls (50,949 dials) while producing only 555 sign-ups.

There are two complementary ways to measure the phone calls saved by enforcing a 3-call limit:

Stopping after attempt 3 on current lists (Truncated view): If agents still dial 3 times on the 7,635 stubborn leads before stopping, we cut 28,044 extra calls (26.5% of all dials).

Filtering out low-odds leads upfront (Cohort view): If we use our pre-call scoring rules to skip those 7,635 low-odds prospects before they enter the repeat loop, we save all 50,949 calls (48.2% of all dials) and 475.2 hours of talk time.

Mobile outreach beats landlines 2.8 to 1, and past buyers convert at 65.1%

Section 04

Our second finding centers on channel selection and customer relationship history. Cellular outreach delivers a massive commercial advantage over traditional landlines, converting at 14.74% compared to 5.23% for landlines (a 2.82x lift, with an age-adjusted odds ratio of 2.90x).

Furthermore, prior campaign interaction is the single strongest commercial signal in the dataset. Customers who agreed to a deposit in a previous campaign converted at 65.11% (36.00% yield per dialed call), representing a 7.37x lift over uncontacted prospects.

When calling someone from an earlier campaign, timing matters. Prospects called within 7 days converted at 65.76%, compared to 56.88% when agents waited 8 to 14 days.

Shifting calls from 31-45 year olds (9.4%) to seniors (45.5%), students (31.4%), and low-rate periods (24.5%)

Section 05

Outbound dialers currently direct 53.1% of all calls (56,174 dials) to core working-age adults (31-45 years old), where conversion averages only 9.37%. Meanwhile, demographic cohorts with the highest affinity for term deposit yields are drastically under-contacted: seniors aged 60+ convert at 45.49%, and students convert at 31.43%.

Simultaneously, macroeconomic interest rate shifts exert massive control over depositor behavior. In low-interest-rate environments (Euribor <= 3.0%), retail depositors actively seek safe returns, driving conversion to 24.46% (11.69% yield per call) compared to just 4.84% during high-rate periods (Euribor > 3.0%).
